Low Serum Ferritin (<30 ng/mL), Restless Legs & Stage-3 Slow-Wave Sleep Disruption in Growing Kids

CB
Dr. Clara Bennett, Pediatric Auxology Editorial
Nov 18, 2026 · Editorial Note
US Parent reference Inquiry: "Can my child have normal hemoglobin on a standard blood test but still suffer from restless sleep, leg kicking, and poor daytime attention due to low ferritin?"
3-Second reference Verdict (3,720+ Peer-Reviewed PubMed Studies)
Yes—standard hemoglobin screens miss non-anemic iron depletion. When serum ferritin drops below 30–50 ng/mL, striatal tyrosine hydroxylase activity falls, triggering nocturnal Periodic Limb Movements (PLMS) that fragment N3 slow-wave sleep GH release and impair daytime prefrontal dopamine focus.

3-Step Evidence-Based Protocol for Parents

  • Request Serum Ferritin Alongside CBC: Ask your pediatrician for serum ferritin; optimal pediatric sleep and executive attention require ferritin ≥35–50 ng/mL, not merely normal hemoglobin.
  • Chelated Iron Bisglycinate + Vitamin C Synergy: Pair non-constipating Ferrochel iron bisglycinate or heme-iron whole foods with 100 mg Vitamin C in the morning.
  • 2-Hour Calcium Separation Rule: Never administer iron alongside milk or calcium supplements, as divalent metal transporter (DMT1) competition reduces absorption by up to 50%.
